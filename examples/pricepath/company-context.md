# Example Company: PricePath

This is a fictional example.

Do not use real customer names, real revenue numbers, or private company data in public examples.

---

## Company

PricePath is a B2B SaaS company that helps mid-market SaaS companies understand revenue operations performance.

The product is used by RevOps, Sales leadership, Customer Success leadership, and Finance teams.

Customers use PricePath to spot pipeline risk, discounting patterns, expansion opportunities, renewal risk, and handoff problems between Sales and CS.

---

## Product

PricePath includes:

- Revenue dashboards
- Pipeline inspection
- Discount analysis
- Renewal and expansion reporting
- Forecast variance tracking
- Account health signals
- CRM and billing integrations
- Executive reporting
- Board-ready revenue summaries

---

## Customer segments

PricePath sells to:

- Early-stage SaaS companies that want basic revenue visibility
- Mid-market SaaS companies with a dedicated RevOps function
- Larger SaaS companies with multiple sales teams, customer segments, and regional processes

The company is sales-led.

Most deals involve RevOps as the champion, Sales or CS leadership as daily stakeholders, and Finance or the COO as the economic buyer.

---

## Current packaging

PricePath has three plans:

- **Monitor** for basic revenue visibility
- **Improve** for operational teams that need analysis and alerts
- **Scale** for larger teams that need forecasting, advanced permissions, and executive reporting

There are also paid add-ons for:

- Additional data sources
- Advanced forecasting
- Custom board reports
- Premium implementation support

The plan names sound simple, but the boundaries are not simple in practice.

Several high-value workflows depend on features split across plans and add-ons.

---

## Current situation

PricePath is seeing more pricing and packaging confusion.

Sales is discounting heavily to close normal mid-market deals.

Customers often ask what is actually included in each plan.

CS says some new customers discover after purchase that the workflow they expected requires an add-on or a higher plan.

Leadership wants to add a new tier quickly to create a clearer upgrade path.

Product needs to decide if the problem is:

- Price level
- Packaging
- Value story
- ICP fit
- Sales enablement
- Onboarding expectation setting
- Something else

---

## Product principles for this example

1. Start with the customer problem, not the pricing table.
2. Do not add a tier until the value boundary is clear.
3. Package around customer outcomes, not internal feature groups.
4. Discounts are signal, not strategy.
5. Sales needs a story that can be explained without a pricing expert.
6. Pricing changes should reduce confusion, not move it somewhere else.
7. Decisions should be written down so the team remembers what it chose not to do.
