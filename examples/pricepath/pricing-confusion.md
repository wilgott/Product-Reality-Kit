# Example: Pricing Confusion

This is a fictional example.

---

## Situation

PricePath sells revenue operations analytics to mid-market SaaS companies.

The product has three plans: Monitor, Improve, and Scale.

The current pricing page looks clean, but deals are getting messy.

Sales says customers do not understand why important workflows are split across plans and add-ons.

Customers say they cannot tell which plan they need without a long call.

CS says new customers sometimes feel surprised when expected reporting workflows require a higher plan or paid add-on.

Leadership wants to add a new tier in the next six weeks.

Product needs to decide if the right move is:

- A new tier
- Lower prices
- Simpler packaging
- Better value story
- Sales enablement
- ICP clarification
- Research before structural pricing changes

---

## What Sales says

> We are spending too much time explaining packaging. Customers understand the product once they see it, but they push back on why forecasting is in Scale, discount analysis is in Improve, and board reports are an add-on. We keep discounting because the package conversation gets awkward.

Sales says discounting has become normal in competitive mid-market deals.

Common Sales objections:

- "This feels expensive for reporting."
- "Why is this not included in the plan we already need?"
- "Can you just include the board report add-on?"
- "Your competitor includes forecasting in the middle plan."
- "We only need this for Sales, not the whole revenue team."

Sales leadership wants a new plan between Improve and Scale.

The proposed plan would include forecasting, discount analysis, and limited executive reporting.

---

## What customers say

Customers do not all describe the problem the same way.

RevOps champions say:

> We need one place to explain pipeline quality, discount leakage, and renewal risk. I do not want to stitch together CRM reports every week.

Sales leaders say:

> I care about forecast confidence and which reps are discounting too much. I do not care what module that lives in.

Finance buyers say:

> I need to know if this replaces manual board reporting or just gives RevOps another dashboard.

CS leaders say:

> Expansion and renewal risk matter to us, but it is unclear whether this is a Sales tool or a revenue leadership tool.

Several buyers ask for a simple recommendation:

> Which plan is for a 150-person SaaS company with a RevOps team, two sales motions, and board reporting pressure?

---

## What CS says

CS says the confusion continues after close.

Recent onboarding issues:

- Customers expected board-ready reporting but bought Improve without the board report add-on.
- Customers expected renewal risk views but only connected CRM data, not billing data.
- Customers expected forecasting workflows but bought Monitor after a discounted deal.
- Admins ask why account health, discount analysis, and forecast variance are separated when their weekly revenue meeting needs all three.

CS says the product can deliver value, but the package does not map cleanly to how customers run revenue meetings.

---

## What leadership says

Leadership wants the team to move quickly.

> We need a cleaner upgrade path. Add a new tier, give Sales something clearer to sell, and stop the discounting.

The CRO thinks a new tier could help Sales land bigger deals.

The CEO worries that PricePath is underpriced for larger customers.

The CFO worries discounting is training customers to wait for end-of-quarter concessions.

Product worries the new tier could make the packaging harder to understand if the underlying value story stays unclear.

---

## Current evidence

What we know:

- Discounting has increased in mid-market deals over the last two quarters.
- Sales says packaging confusion appears in many late-stage opportunities.
- CS has logged onboarding surprises related to add-ons and plan boundaries.
- Win/loss notes mention "unclear package fit" more often than "too expensive."
- Customers with both Sales and CS leadership involved expand more often than customers buying for Sales alone.
- The highest-retention customers use PricePath in weekly revenue leadership meetings, not only in RevOps reporting.
- Competitors package similar capabilities differently, especially forecasting and executive reporting.

What we do not know:

- Whether the main issue is price, packaging, positioning, ICP, or Sales enablement.
- Which workflows customers believe are core versus premium.
- Whether customers would pay more for a clearer revenue leadership package.
- Whether the current plan names make sense to buyers.
- Whether discounting is caused by weak value story, wrong prospects, competitor pressure, or poor package fit.
- Whether a new tier would reduce confusion or create another boundary to explain.

---

## Pressure

The pressure is real.

Discounting is hurting revenue quality.

Sales needs a clearer story.

CS needs fewer expectation gaps during onboarding.

Leadership wants action this quarter.

But the team should not assume the answer is a new tier.

If customers are confused about the value boundary, another tier may only create another argument.
