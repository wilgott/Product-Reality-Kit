# Example Product Decision Record

This is a fictional example.

---

# PDR-002: Do not add a new pricing tier until package confusion is understood

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-06-23

## Owner

Product

---

## Decision

We will not add a new tier between Improve and Scale in the next six weeks.

We will clarify PricePath's packaging and value story, improve Sales enablement for the current plans, and run focused research before making structural pricing changes.

---

## Customer problem

Revenue leaders at mid-market SaaS companies need a clear way to understand pipeline quality, discount leakage, forecast confidence, renewal risk, and executive reporting in one operating rhythm.

They need to know which PricePath plan supports that job without decoding internal feature boundaries.

The signal is credible.

The proposed new tier is not validated yet.

---

## Context

PricePath is seeing more pricing and packaging confusion.

Sales is discounting more often in mid-market deals.

Customers ask what is included in each plan and why important workflows are split across plans and add-ons.

CS reports onboarding surprises when customers learn that expected reporting workflows require a higher plan or paid add-on.

Leadership wants to add a new tier quickly to give Sales a cleaner upgrade path.

---

## What we know

- Discounting has increased in mid-market deals over the last two quarters.
- Sales says plan boundaries are hard to explain in late-stage opportunities.
- Customers ask which plan fits common mid-market situations.
- CS has logged onboarding surprises around add-ons and plan limits.
- Win/loss notes mention unclear package fit more often than price alone.
- High-retention customers use PricePath in weekly revenue leadership meetings.
- Customers with both Sales and CS leadership involved expand more often than customers buying for Sales alone.
- Competitors package forecasting and executive reporting differently.

---

## What we do not know yet

- We do not know if the main issue is price, packaging, positioning, Sales enablement, onboarding, or ICP.
- We do not know which workflows customers see as core versus premium.
- We do not know whether a new tier would reduce discounting.
- We do not know if the current plan names match how buyers think about value.
- We do not know whether some discounted deals are poor ICP fits.
- We do not know if customers would pay more for a clearer revenue leadership package.

---

## Options considered

### Option A: Add the new tier now

Pros:

- Gives Sales a new package to sell quickly.
- May help with buyers who sit between Improve and Scale.
- Shows leadership visible action this quarter.

Cons:

- May create another unclear boundary.
- May not reduce discounting if the value story remains weak.
- Could make CS onboarding harder.
- Could require a larger pricing cleanup later.
- May encode internal assumptions before customer value is understood.

### Option B: Lower prices or allow heavier discounting

Pros:

- May close some price-sensitive deals.
- Gives Sales short-term flexibility.
- Does not require immediate packaging work.

Cons:

- Trains customers to negotiate.
- Hurts revenue quality.
- Does not fix confusing plan boundaries.
- May hide weak positioning or poor ICP fit.

### Option C: Clarify packaging and value story before structural pricing changes

Pros:

- Addresses the confusion directly.
- Can improve Sales conversations before changing the pricing model.
- Helps identify which workflows are core, premium, or add-on.
- Reduces the risk of creating the wrong tier.
- Gives leadership a clearer basis for a later pricing decision.

Cons:

- Does not give Sales a new SKU immediately.
- May not reduce discounting in the current quarter.
- Requires Product, Sales, CS, and Finance to align on evidence.

### Option D: Narrow ICP and qualification

Pros:

- Reduces bad-fit deals that need heavy discounting.
- Helps Sales focus on customers who value the full revenue operating workflow.
- May improve retention and expansion quality.

Cons:

- May reduce near-term pipeline.
- Does not fully solve package confusion for good-fit customers.
- Requires leadership discipline when pipeline is under pressure.

---

## Trade-off

We accept that Sales will not get a new tier this quarter.

We also accept that some discount pressure may continue while research and enablement work happen.

We are choosing not to make the pricing structure more complex before we know what customers are actually confused about.

---

## What we are saying no to

- We are saying no to adding a tier before the value boundary is clear.
- We are saying no to treating discounting as proof that the price is too high.
- We are saying no to using discounts as the main answer to package confusion.
- We are saying no to moving features between plans based only on internal opinion.
- We are saying no to changing pricing faster than Sales and CS can explain it.

---

## What we are saying yes to

- We are saying yes to taking pricing confusion seriously.
- We are saying yes to researching what customers think they are buying.
- We are saying yes to improving the plan story for Sales now.
- We are saying yes to reviewing win/loss notes, discount data, onboarding surprises, and expansion patterns.
- We are saying yes to considering structural packaging changes after the evidence is clearer.

---

## Reversal criteria

We will reconsider adding a new tier if:

- Customer research shows a clear mid-market segment that consistently needs a bundle between Improve and Scale.
- Discount analysis shows heavy discounting is concentrated in deals that fit that segment.
- Win/loss notes show the same missing package boundary across a meaningful share of qualified deals.
- Sales can explain the proposed tier in one minute without exceptions.
- CS confirms the proposed tier would reduce onboarding surprises.
- Finance confirms the expected revenue impact justifies the operational change.

---

## Review date

Review in 30 days.

Before review, complete:

- 8 customer or prospect interviews across won, lost, and expanded accounts
- Discount pattern analysis by segment, plan, rep, competitor, and quarter timing
- Review of the last 20 win/loss notes where pricing or packaging was mentioned
- CS review of onboarding surprises from the last 10 new customers
- Draft one-minute Sales narrative for each current plan
- Finance review of revenue impact scenarios for any proposed packaging change

---

## Communication plan

### Sales

Sales should not promise a new tier this quarter.

Sales can say:

> We are clarifying PricePath's packaging around the revenue workflows customers actually run. We are not adding another tier until we know it will reduce confusion rather than create a new boundary to explain.

### CS

CS should collect onboarding surprises related to plan boundaries, add-ons, and expected workflows.

CS should identify customers who are getting strong value and document what they understood before buying.

### Finance

Finance should analyze discount patterns and model the revenue impact of possible packaging changes.

### Marketing

Marketing should help test whether the current plan story explains customer outcomes clearly.

### Leadership

Leadership should understand the trade-off:

> A new tier may look decisive, but it could make the packaging harder to explain if the team has not identified the real value boundary.

---

## Notes

This decision does not mean PricePath's pricing is fine.

It means the team should diagnose the confusion before changing the structure.
