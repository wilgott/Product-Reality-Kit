# Example: Diagnostic Output

This is a fictional example based on the pricing confusion situation in this folder.

Use it to see how a messy pricing discussion can become a clearer product decision.

---

## Situation

PricePath customers and Sales are confused by plan boundaries.

Discounting is increasing.

CS is seeing onboarding surprises when customers learn that expected workflows require a higher plan or paid add-on.

Leadership wants to add a new tier quickly.

---

## Diagnostic used

- [When Pricing Is Confusing](../../diagnostics/when-pricing-is-confusing.md)

---

## The request

> Add a new tier between Improve and Scale so Sales has a clearer package to sell and discounting slows down.

---

## The likely customer problem

Revenue leaders at mid-market SaaS companies need a clear way to understand pipeline quality, discount leakage, forecast confidence, renewal risk, and executive reporting in one operating rhythm.

They are not trying to buy separate internal modules.

They are trying to run better revenue meetings with less manual reporting and fewer surprises.

The current package boundaries may not match that job.

---

## What looks real

There is a real problem.

Evidence:

- Sales is spending too much time explaining plan boundaries.
- Discounting has increased in mid-market deals.
- Customers ask what they actually get without a long call.
- CS has logged onboarding surprises around add-ons and plan limits.
- Win/loss notes mention unclear package fit more often than price alone.
- High-retention customers use PricePath across revenue leadership, not only RevOps reporting.

This is worth acting on.

The action should start with understanding the confusion, not adding another tier by default.

---

## What looks risky

The proposed solution may treat the symptom.

Risks:

- A new tier may create another boundary Sales has to explain.
- The team may mistake discounting for a pure price problem.
- Sales may keep discounting if the value story remains unclear.
- Customers may still feel punished if core workflows depend on add-ons.
- CS may inherit more expectation gaps after close.
- Leadership may move pricing structure faster than the team understands customer value.
- The package may still reflect internal feature groups instead of customer outcomes.

---

## Possible false diagnoses

### "We need lower prices."

Maybe.

But customers may be pushing back because they do not understand what outcome they are buying.

If the value story is weak, lower prices only make the weak story cheaper.

### "We need another tier."

Maybe.

But another tier can make confusion worse if the existing plan boundaries are already unclear.

The team should know which customer job the new tier would solve before creating it.

### "Sales just needs to hold the line."

Maybe Sales discipline is part of the problem.

But Sales may be discounting because they are forced to defend package boundaries that do not match how buyers think.

### "This is just a pricing page problem."

Maybe the page needs work.

But the confusion appears in sales calls, onboarding, and win/loss notes.

That points to packaging, value story, enablement, or ICP, not only website copy.

---

## Questions to ask next

### Ask customers

- What revenue meeting or decision are you trying to improve with PricePath?
- Which workflows did you assume were included when you bought?
- Which capabilities feel core to the value of the product?
- Which capabilities feel advanced or optional?
- What would make one plan clearly better than another?
- What alternative are you comparing PricePath against?
- Who needs to understand the value for the deal to move forward?
- What would make the product worth more to your leadership team?

### Ask Sales

- Which plan boundary is hardest to explain?
- Which objections lead to discounting most often?
- Are discounts concentrated by segment, deal size, competitor, rep, or quarter timing?
- What exact value story is Sales using for each plan?
- Can reps explain the package in one minute without exceptions?
- Which prospects are a poor ICP fit but still entering late-stage pipeline?
- What promises are being made about add-ons during negotiation?

### Ask CS

- Which expectation gaps appear during onboarding?
- Which features do customers believe are required for first value?
- Which package surprises create support or renewal risk?
- What workarounds are customers using when they bought the wrong plan?
- Which customers expand successfully, and what did they understand before buying?

### Ask Finance and leadership

- Is the main concern discount rate, average contract value, expansion, churn, or sales cycle length?
- Which metric would prove the packaging change worked?
- How much revenue risk are we willing to accept while researching?
- What pricing changes would require board or customer communication?

---

## Decision options

### Option A: Add the new tier now

Pros:

- Gives Sales a new package to sell quickly.
- May capture buyers who sit between Improve and Scale.
- Shows leadership visible action this quarter.

Cons:

- May add complexity before the team understands the real confusion.
- May create another unclear boundary.
- May not reduce discounting if the value story remains weak.
- May require later cleanup that is harder to explain to customers.

### Option B: Lower prices or increase discount authority

Pros:

- May help close price-sensitive deals.
- Reduces short-term friction for Sales.
- Requires less packaging work immediately.

Cons:

- Trains customers to negotiate.
- Does not fix unclear package value.
- May hurt revenue quality.
- May hide ICP or positioning problems.

### Option C: Clarify packaging and value story before structural pricing changes

Pros:

- Attacks the confusion directly.
- Can improve Sales conversations quickly without changing contracts.
- Helps the team learn which capabilities customers see as core, premium, or add-on.
- Reduces the risk of creating the wrong tier.

Cons:

- Does not give leadership an immediate new SKU.
- May not solve all discounting quickly.
- Requires focused research and Sales/CS alignment.

### Option D: Narrow the ICP and improve qualification

Pros:

- May reduce bad-fit deals that need heavy discounting.
- Helps Sales focus on customers who value the full revenue operating workflow.
- Could improve conversion and retention quality.

Cons:

- May reduce near-term pipeline.
- Does not by itself fix package confusion for good-fit customers.
- Requires leadership discipline.

---

## Recommended decision

Do not add a new tier in the next six weeks.

Clarify the packaging and value story first.

Run focused research with customers, recent losses, Sales, and CS to identify which workflows are core, which are premium, and which are add-ons.

Create updated Sales enablement for the current plans while research runs.

Review structural pricing changes after the team has evidence.

---

## What we are saying no to

- No to adding a tier before the value boundary is clear.
- No to treating discounting as proof that the price is too high.
- No to moving features between plans based only on internal opinion.
- No to letting Sales solve package confusion through discounts.
- No to changing pricing faster than the team can explain the change.

---

## What we are saying yes to

- Yes to taking Sales and customer confusion seriously.
- Yes to researching what customers think they are buying.
- Yes to improving the plan story and Sales enablement now.
- Yes to checking discount patterns, win/loss notes, and onboarding surprises.
- Yes to considering packaging changes after the evidence is clearer.
