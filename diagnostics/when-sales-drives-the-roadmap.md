# When Sales Drives the Roadmap

Sales input is important.

But Sales should not own the roadmap.

Use this diagnostic when Sales, leadership, or a large customer is pushing hard for a feature.

The goal is not to block Sales.

The goal is to turn sales pressure into better product decisions.

---

## When to use this

Use this when you hear things like:

- "We need this to close the deal."
- "The customer already expects it."
- "Enterprise needs this."
- "The competitor has it."
- "It is just a small thing."
- "Can we commit it this quarter?"
- "Sales cannot sell without this."

---

## The uncomfortable truth

A sales request can be a strong market signal.

It can also be a one-off request dressed up as strategy.

Your job is not to say yes or no too fast.

Your job is to understand what is behind the request.

---

## First questions

Start here:

1. What customer problem is behind the request?
2. Is the customer asking for the solution, or describing the problem?
3. Is this request coming from one customer, one segment, or the market?
4. Does this match our ideal customer profile?
5. Would this help us win more of the customers we actually want?
6. Would existing customers use this?
7. Would Sales be able to sell this again?
8. Is this a product gap, a pricing gap, a packaging gap, or a GTM gap?
9. What happens if we do not build it?
10. What would we stop doing if we say yes?

---

## Red flags

Be careful when you hear:

- "They will sign if we build this."
- "We already promised it."
- "It is just configuration."
- "This is only for one customer, but they are important."
- "We do not have time for discovery."
- "Everyone in enterprise needs this." Without evidence.
- "The competitor has it." Without understanding why.
- "This is a blocker." Without knowing blocked from what.

---

## Common false diagnoses

### "We need this feature to sell."

Maybe.

But it could also mean:

- Sales is targeting the wrong customer.
- The current value is not clear enough.
- The package is hard to understand.
- The demo is weak.
- The competitor is being used as pressure.
- The buyer wants a checkbox, not real product value.

### "Enterprise needs this."

Which enterprise?

All enterprise customers are not the same.

One large customer is not a market.

### "It is just a small feature."

Small to build is not the same as small to maintain, sell, support, explain, and onboard.

---

## What to check

Before deciding, check:

- Number of customers or prospects asking for it
- Revenue at risk or revenue opportunity
- Segment fit
- ICP fit
- Strategic fit
- Support and CS feedback
- Win/loss data
- Existing workarounds
- Product complexity
- Engineering cost
- Maintenance cost
- GTM impact
- Packaging impact

---

## Decision options

You usually have more options than "build" or "do not build".

Possible decisions:

- Build now
- Build later
- Run discovery first
- Offer a workaround
- Solve the wider problem in a generic way
- Make it a paid add-on
- Handle it through implementation or services
- Change pricing or packaging
- Improve Sales enablement
- Say no

---

## Product Decision Record output

After the diagnostic, write a Product Decision Record.

Recommended template:

- [Standard PDR](../decision-records/product-decision-record.md)

Make sure the decision record includes:

- The real customer problem
- Why the request came up now
- The evidence
- The options considered
- The trade-off
- What you are saying no to
- When to review the decision again
