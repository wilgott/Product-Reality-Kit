# When Pricing Is Confusing

Pricing confusion is not always a pricing problem.

Sometimes the price is wrong.

Sometimes the value is unclear.

Sometimes the package is too hard to explain.

Sometimes Sales is discounting because the product story is weak.

Use this diagnostic when customers, Sales, CS, or leadership are confused about what the product costs, what is included, or why it is worth paying for.

---

## When to use this

Use this when you hear things like:

- "Customers do not understand the pricing."
- "Sales needs too many discounts."
- "This should be in another tier."
- "Nobody knows what is included."
- "Customers compare us to cheaper tools."
- "Expansion is harder than it should be."
- "We need a new package."
- "This feature should be an add-on."

---

## The uncomfortable truth

Customers do not buy your internal product structure.

They buy outcomes, confidence, speed, risk reduction, revenue, control, compliance, or saved work.

If the package does not match how customers understand value, pricing will feel confusing even if the numbers are reasonable.

---

## First questions

Start here:

1. Who is confused: buyer, user, admin, Sales, CS, or leadership?
2. What exactly is confusing: price, package, limits, usage, add-ons, contract terms, or value?
3. Are we pricing based on how customers receive value?
4. Are we packaging based on customer outcomes or internal product areas?
5. Can Sales explain the package in one minute?
6. Can customers understand what they get without a long call?
7. Are the most valuable features easy to find and easy to explain?
8. Are discounts hiding a value problem?
9. Are customers comparing us to the right alternatives?
10. Is this a pricing problem, packaging problem, positioning problem, onboarding problem, or ICP problem?

---

## Red flags

Be careful when:

- Sales needs heavy discounting to close normal deals.
- Customers ask "what do I actually get?"
- The package is based on internal modules instead of customer value.
- Important features are hidden in strange tiers.
- Add-ons solve problems customers expect in the core product.
- Expansion depends on manual explanation from Sales or CS.
- The pricing page creates more questions than answers.
- Nobody can explain why one plan costs more than another.
- The team wants to change pricing without understanding the value problem.

---

## Common false diagnoses

### "We need lower prices."

Maybe.

But it could also mean:

- Customers do not understand the value.
- Sales is selling to the wrong segment.
- The package is too complex.
- The problem is not painful enough.
- The buyer is not the person who feels the pain.
- The product takes too long to show value.

### "We need another tier."

Maybe.

But another tier can make the problem worse if the value story is already unclear.

More packages do not automatically create more clarity.

### "This should be an add-on."

Maybe.

But be careful.

If the add-on is needed to get core value, customers may feel punished for buying the product.

---

## What to check

Before deciding, check:

- Win/loss notes
- Discount patterns
- Deal cycle length
- Expansion data
- Churn reasons
- Support and CS feedback
- Sales objections
- Feature usage by plan
- Customer segment differences
- Competitor positioning
- Pricing page questions
- Onboarding drop-offs
- Time to value
- What customers think they are buying

---

## Decision options

Possible decisions:

- Keep pricing as-is and improve positioning
- Rename packages
- Simplify tiers
- Move features between plans
- Create an add-on
- Remove an add-on
- Change usage limits
- Improve Sales enablement
- Improve onboarding
- Narrow the ICP
- Run willingness-to-pay research
- Delay pricing changes until the value story is clearer

---

## Product Decision Record output

After the diagnostic, write a Product Decision Record.

Recommended template:

- [Standard PDR](../decision-records/product-decision-record.md)

Make sure the decision record includes:

- What confusion you are trying to fix
- Which customer segment is affected
- What evidence you have
- Why this is pricing, packaging, positioning, onboarding, or ICP
- What trade-off you are accepting
- What you are saying no to

## Simple rule

Do not change pricing just because pricing feels messy.

Find out what is actually confusing first.
